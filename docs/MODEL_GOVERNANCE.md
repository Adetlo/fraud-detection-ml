# Model governance

A governance record for the `fraud-risk/1.0.0` scoring model. It follows the shape of a
mature model-risk document without claiming to satisfy any specific regulator, standard or
internal policy.

## 1. Purpose

Rank synthetic financial transactions by fraud risk and attach a decision-support label
(PASS / REVIEW / PRIORITY_REVIEW) and risk indicators, so that a downstream human or
control process can prioritise review effort.

## 2. Intended use

* Educational demonstration of fraud risk scoring and ML engineering practice.
* Decision *support*: the output orders work for a reviewer or feeds a downstream control
  that applies its own rules.
* Batch and online scoring of records that satisfy `fraud-transaction/1`.

## 3. Prohibited use

* Autonomous blocking, declining or reversing of transactions.
* Any decision affecting a real person or account.
* Regulatory reporting, compliance evidence or suspicious-activity determinations.
* Scoring data that does not satisfy the contract, or data whose history fields were not
  computed point-in-time.

## 4. Training data

* Generated by `fraud_ml.data.synthetic` (seeded, reproducible). Demo: 50,000 transactions,
  1,250 accounts, 181 days, 1.00% fraud, five synthetic fraud types produced by ten
  stylised patterns.
* Lineage: `metadata.json` records `training_data_version` (SHA-256 of the source file),
  the source filename, row count, seed, library versions, git commit (when trained in a
  checkout) and the full training configuration.
* Labels are generator conventions, not investigated outcomes.

## 5. Features and leakage controls

37 features in four groups ([FEATURE_ENGINEERING.md](FEATURE_ENGINEERING.md)). Labels,
identifiers, raw timestamps and post-outcome fields are forbidden from the feature matrix,
history features use strictly earlier rows, and preprocessing is fitted on the training
split only. Each control has an automated test.

## 6. Evaluation approach

Temporal 70/15/15 split; PR-AUC as the primary ranking metric; precision, recall, F1,
false-positive rate, Brier, review rate, alerts per 1,000 and illustrative expected cost at
the selected threshold; segment performance by currency, channel, transaction type and
domestic/cross-border. Selection decisions use the validation split only; the test split is
reported once ([MODEL_EVALUATION.md](MODEL_EVALUATION.md)).

## 7. Threshold policy

Recall target 0.80 within a 5% review-rate ceiling, lowest illustrative cost among feasible
thresholds; a priority threshold at 0.75 precision. The policy and its satisfaction flag are
persisted with the artifact ([THRESHOLDING.md](THRESHOLDING.md)).

## 8. Known limitations

Synthetic data and labels; no real behaviour, seasonality or adversarial adaptation; no
analyst feedback; cold-start history at the window start; small positive counts make
validation/test metrics noisy (±0.05 PR-AUC); the baseline is competitive out of sample;
no graph or network features; static illustrative FX factors; single-currency account
statistics; reason codes are indicators, not causes. Full list in the README.

## 9. Human oversight

The system never emits a blocking decision. HIGH and CRITICAL bands are recommendations
for review ordering. Reason codes give the reviewer a starting point; the model's own
importance is reported separately as an aggregate. Any real use would require a documented
review workflow, override capability and feedback capture.

## 10. Versioning

* `model_version` (`fraud-risk/1.0.0`): semantic version of the model line, set at training.
* `run_id`: UTC timestamp of the training run; one artifact directory per run; `latest` is
  an explicit promotion (copy).
* `schema_version`, `feature_version`, `score_schema_version`: compatibility gates checked
  at load time.
* `artifact_sha256`: integrity of the serialized pipeline.
* Scores carry `model_version` and `threshold`, so every decision can be traced to the
  artifact and policy that produced it.

## 11. Monitoring

Label-free data and score drift (PSI), score distribution summaries, data-quality snapshots
of scoring batches, structured logs with correlation ids ([DRIFT.md](DRIFT.md)).
Performance drift requires labels and is not automated.

## 12. Retraining considerations

Retrain when: drift status is DRIFT on inputs that carry importance; the score distribution
or review rate moves materially; labelled evaluation on a newer period shows recall or
precision below policy; the contract or feature catalogue changes (new feature version).
Retraining must keep the temporal protocol, re-run the baseline comparison and re-select
the threshold; the new run gets a new `run_id` and is promoted explicitly.

## 13. Rollback considerations

Every run is kept in its own directory with full metadata. Rollback is copying a previous
run to `latest` (or pointing `FRAUD_ML_MODEL_DIR` at a directory whose `latest` is the
previous run) and restarting the service; `/health/ready` and `/api/v1/model` confirm the
active version. Because policy travels with the artifact, rolling back the model also rolls
back its threshold and bands.
